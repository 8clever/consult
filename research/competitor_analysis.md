# Competitor Analysis: McKinsey vs BCG vs Bain

> Research date: Aug 25, 2026
> Purpose: extract design, messaging, and conversion patterns from the Big-3 consulting websites to inform the Vitiaev Intelligence one-page landing.
> Sources: live sites (mckinsey.com, bcg.com, bain.com), UX case studies by The Story (66–67 best practices per site), Phi Web Studio B2B consultant website guide 2026, Framer "Strategy Consulting Website Design" guide (May 2026).

---

## 1. Side-by-Side Comparison

| Aspect | McKinsey & Company | Boston Consulting Group | Bain & Company |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Core model** | Thought leadership as brand engine. Homepage is ~80% links to research/articles; services are secondary | Insights bridged to outcomes. Current-era positioning front and center ("Where Strategic Clarity Meets Applied AI") | Outcome-first messaging wrapped in warmth; cleanest scan path of the three |
| **Hero** | Rotating featured research + tagline ("we help you think bigger, build stronger") | Positioning statement tied to the dominant market theme (Applied AI) | Client-outcome statements, minimal text |
| **Navigation logic** | By capability: Industries / Functions / How we help clients | By theme and client result | By result the client wants |
| **Content role** | The product IS the content (MGI reports, surveys). Content earns authority that sells consulting | Reports framed as decisions pending right now | Reports framed as results already achieved |
| **Typography** | Serif headlines (editorial, Bower typeface), sans-serif UI text | Sans-serif, high-contrast display | Sans-serif, generous whitespace |
| **Palette** | Deep navy on white — trust-coded blue system | Dark teal/black with bright accent moments | Warm neutrals with a single signature color |
| **Imagery** | Editorial photography of people/places, zero stock-SaaS tropes | Abstract data visuals | Human-centered, restrained |
| **CTA style** | Soft: "Explore insights". No hard sell anywhere | Medium: "See how we work" | Soft-mid: contact routed per practice |
| **What the site optimizes for** | Authority maintenance + talent attraction (careers block on homepage) | Relevance to the AI moment | Clarity for a scanning executive |

UX audit scores (The Story, Nielsen heuristics): McKinsey homepage — 10 good practices, 0 errors; BCG — 66 best practices cataloged; Bain — 67 best practices cataloged.

## 2. What Each Firm Does Best

### McKinsey
1. **Publishes like a media company.** Original research (McKinsey Global Institute) creates permanent inbound assets; frameworks (7S) compound for decades.
2. **Editorial restraint signals seniority.** Serif type, white space, disciplined navy palette — reads like a publication, not a SaaS landing.
3. **Zero visual noise.** No gradients, no animation-heavy heroes, no stock tropes.

**Limitation for us:** their site does not need to convert visitors — the brand closes deals offline. A copy-paste of their content-first homepage would starve an unknown personal brand of leads.

### BCG
1. **Positions against the current wave.** "Strategic Clarity Meets Applied AI" — they rename themselves around what buyers are worried about *right now*.
2. **Bridges insight → outcome explicitly.** Every report teaser answers "so what".
3. **Reports presented as urgent decisions**, not archives.

### Bain
1. **Outcome language instead of methodology talk.** Not "we do strategy consulting" but "the specific result clients achieve".
2. **Best scan economy.** Minimal text, color used to direct attention — built for a C-level 10-second skim.
3. **Warmth without losing authority** — the most approachable of the three.

---

## 3. Patterns All Three Share (Adopt)

1. **Editorial typography over product typography** — serif headlines, generous spacing, restrained palette. Restraint = seniority = price justification.
2. **Named people, not faceless firm** — partners with photos and points of view. C-level buyers want to know who they're paying.
3. **Original frameworks/IP as durable assets** — named methods outlive campaigns.
4. **One primary action per page**, softly repeated — never competing CTAs.
5. **Performance discipline** — executives open several firm sites in tabs during procurement; slow loses before reading starts.
6. **Specificity everywhere** — named industries, companies, dollar amounts. Generic phrases ("unlock value", "strategic alignment") read as filler and damage credibility.

## 4. What NOT to Copy (Reject)

1. **Content-first homepage** — works only when everyone already knows you. An unknown brand must state who it helps and with what outcome in the first screen.
2. **Careers/talent blocks** — irrelevant for a solo practice.
3. **Soft-only CTAs** — big-3 can afford "explore"; we need explicit "Request pricing / Book intro call" paths.
4. **Archive-style report libraries** — they give research away to sell consulting; we sell the intelligence itself.

## 5. Translation to the Vitiaev Intelligence Landing

| Big-3 pattern | Our adaptation |
|---|---|
| Editorial serif restraint | Serif display font (Newsreader) + ink-navy/bronze palette; no gradients, no SaaS tropes |
| BCG's "positioned on the current wave" | Hero speaks to agentic-AI-era buying: knowing what business needs before competitors do |
| Bain's outcome language | Every section answers "what does the buyer get": time back for sales teams, pain-matched outreach, region-entry decisions |
| Named partner credibility | Analyst note signed by a practicing CTO — signals read as an engineer reads them |
| Specificity | Report contents listed concretely (named leads, dated post links, intent signals, prioritized outreach list) — mirrors the real deliverable format |
| One primary CTA | "Request pricing" repeated nav → mid → final; reply-within-one-business-day promise (big-3 service standard) |
| Framework/IP habit | "Two products, one system" framing: Reports × CTO judgment = decisions — a ownable mental model |
| Their weakness (no urgency, no limits statement) | "Scoped to your question" section: any region, industry, account list, or technology — flexibility the giants' fixed taxonomies can't offer |
